Findings from reconstructing a Rivian R2 as procedural three.js geometry, written as inputs for a reusable Skill. Everything here is a conclusion drawn from something that actually went wrong or actually worked in the build — not a plan for how it might go.

01

The finding that dominates all the others

Finding Vision review does not converge. Measurement does.

Across this build, every defect that survived more than one round survived because it was being judged by looking at a picture. Every one of them fell out in a single pass the moment it was turned into a number.

Defects by rounds of visual review survived
DefectRoundsHow it actually resolved
Windscreen base 111 mm too far forward One silhouette measurement against the official drawing
A‑pillar “too thick” The thick edge was the far pillar’s shadow through the glass — a fact about the image, not the model
Windscreen “hangs over the cowl” Profiled the silhouette column by column: 21 mm tall at the tip, split into two rims
Side windows “have a seam” Counted background pixels inside the silhouette: a 12.8 mm hole, exact height printed
Roof 15 mm low The measurement was wrong, not the model — see finding 03
Bar across both door openings Isolating the body shell: four captures, no reasoning
Rear lamp band “inside out” Arithmetic on five offsets against one occluder plane

The productive loop is render → extract silhouette → calibrate to known dimensions → diff in millimetres → edit. The unproductive loop is render → look → describe → edit. A Skill should make the first loop the default and treat screenshots as a final acceptance check, not as the working instrument.

Concretely: the first tool call after any geometry edit should be a numeric check, not a screenshot. Screenshots are for “does this read as a car”, never for “is this edge in the right place”.

02

Reference sources are not interchangeable

  1. Manufacturer orthographic dimension drawings. Vector-rendered, no perspective, and they carry their own dimension callouts with extension lines — so the drawing calibrates itself. The only source worth fitting curves to directly.
  2. Published specification tables. Exact but sparse: length, width, height, wheelbase, track, overhangs, clearance, tyre size. Anchors, never shape.
  3. Factory / body‑in‑white photographs. The best structural evidence available — they show how panels actually meet, with no paint or reflection to hide a joint. Read topology from them, not dimensions.
  4. Press and configurator photography. Good for surface character, actively misleading for measurement: bright glass, studio reflections and long lenses all move edges.
  5. Renders and fan art. Do not use. They inherit someone else’s errors.

The trap, found the hard way: the official drawings were self-consistent in wheelbase and height to 0.7% but drawn about 5% short overall, and the front drawing’s width calibration was internally inconsistent — front tyres scaling to 2072 mm against a 1895 mm spec, and the front track drawn wider than the rear, which is false for this vehicle.

Rule Measure at least two known dimensions from every reference and confirm they agree before fitting anything to it. Even tier 1.

Where they disagree, anchor on the ones that agree and warp the rest. This build anchored y on ground and roof, x on the two axles, and stretched only the overhangs to reach the published length. Shape preserved, known dimensions met.

03

Calibrate on landmarks that cannot move

This produced the single worst measurement error in the build, and it is worth stating as a rule because it is so easy to get wrong.

Calibrating a rendered silhouette by its overall length is wrong. Bumpers, cladding and trim project past the body datum — about 35 mm here — which inflates px/m by 0.75% and silently shortens every height by 13 mm. More than enough to invent a roof defect that does not exist. A full round went to chasing exactly that phantom.

Calibrate instead on landmarks that are definitionally exact:

  • The two axle centres are the published wheelbase apart. Render a wheels-only silhouette (?only=wheelFL,wheelFR,wheelRL,wheelRR) so they separate cleanly from the body.
  • The tyre contact patch is the ground plane, y = 0.
  • Never the overall bounding box, in either axis.
Same geometry, two calibrations
Calibrated onMean deviationWorstVerdict
Overall silhouette length19.4 mm454 mmFiction
Axle centres & contact patch3.4 mm5 mmTrue
Rule Every measurement harness needs a self-test. measure(front wheel → rear wheel) must return the published wheelbase. If it does not, the calibration is broken and every other number it produces is fiction.
04

Turn the renderer into a measuring instrument

The renderer already knows everything; it just has to be asked in a form that returns numbers. Four capabilities paid for themselves many times over and belong in the scaffolding from the first commit, not bolted on at the end.

a · Silhouette mode

?sil=1 renders every surface as flat black on the paper. A clean binary mask with no shading, no hatching and no ink lines to threshold around. Everything else here is built on it.

b · Part isolation

?only=a,b / ?hide=a,b is the bisection tool. When the windscreen “overhang” appeared, four isolation captures found the surface that owned it. Before that, far longer had gone into reasoning about which of eight candidate surfaces it might be, arriving at two confident wrong answers.

RuleBisect, do not deduce.

A detail that cost a round: the isolation flag has to survive the per-frame update loop. The first version set group.visible = false at startup and the animation loop overwrote it on frame one, so two “different” captures came back identical — nearly leading to the conclusion that the surface was somewhere else entirely. Isolation must be a set the update loop consults, not a one-shot write.

c · Deterministic capture

?snap=1&adv=<seconds>&run=0&nodrift=1 jumps the camera with no tween, advances the clock synchronously, and freezes idle animation. Not optional, for two reasons: without it captures are not comparable frame to frame; and the idle animation moves the model — this build bobs the body ±6 mm and the wheels ±8 mm, the same order as the errors being chased. A measurement capture that leaves the animation running is measuring noise.

d · A hole detector

The single highest-value check in the whole build, and about fifteen lines:

silhouette = fill_holes(render_with_sil_1 < threshold)
interior   = binary_erosion(silhouette, 7x7)
holes      = (pixels matching the paper colour) & interior
# label, then report each blob's size and its world-space y range

Any background-coloured pixel strictly inside the vehicle’s own silhouette is a place you can see straight through the car. This turned “the windows have a slight seam” — which had survived three rounds of looking — into 12.8 mm gap, world y 1.1812 to 1.1919, on every door, which is directly actionable. Then it tracked the repair:

Reported1 855 pxSee-through pixels inside the silhouette
After glass to floor1 172 pxDoor glazing closed
After quarter ramp366 pxRemainder is a 1 px hood shut line — a real 6 mm panel gap

Run it after every geometry change. It catches the entire class of defect that separate surfaces produce when they are supposed to meet.

05

Reduce the round trips

Four answers, in order of payoff.

Print numbers, not pictures

A column-by-column profile of a silhouette is a handful of lines of text that says exactly where a surface starts, stops and splits:

  x       segments (m)                 h(mm)
+0.939  1.188-1.168                    21.3     <- lone rim: this is the overhang
+0.914  1.258-1.258 | 1.194-1.173      87.4     <- glass and rim are 70 mm apart
+0.862  1.292-1.258 | 1.209-1.179     115.1

That diagnosis is complete. No screenshot was needed to reach it, and no screenshot could have produced it.

Batch the captures

Never one render, one look, one edit. Capture the whole set — six views plus every motion state — in one command, then read them together. Wall-clock time is dominated by process startup, not by rendering.

Crop before looking

A 3360×2100 sheet reviewed whole shows nothing. Crop to the region in question and upscale with nearest-neighbour so pixel boundaries stay visible. Several defects here were only legible at 3× zoom, and one — a bright lip along the belt line — was invisible at 1×.

Make the model queryable

The biggest structural win: expose the scene graph as callable tools, so the answer to “how big is the battery pack” is a JSON call rather than a render plus a look plus a guess. Bounding boxes, centres, part lists and part-to-part distances are all exact and all free.

06

Can the grid lines help?

Yes, in one specific way, and it is worth being precise about which.

The blueprint sheet’s own grid is drawn in device pixels in screen space, so it is a scale bar for the image, not for the world — useless for measurement. It is, however, an excellent regression tripwire: a known, exact pattern, so any moiré or aliasing in a downscaled screenshot shows up in the grid first. Twice in one session a small-viewport screenshot looked catastrophically broken — dark field, dense speckle — and the grid’s behaviour identified it as a downscaling artefact rather than a rendering failure, confirmed by capturing the same frame at full resolution.

What is genuinely useful is a world-space grid: a ground plane ruled at exactly 1 m via ray/plane intersection through the inverse view-projection. That gives a scale reference that lives in the scene, is correct under both perspective and orthographic projection, and lets a reviewer sanity-check a dimension by eye with no tooling at all. A Skill should include one from the start and, for measurement captures, offer a calibration overlay: the world grid plus a 1 m rule pinned to the ground plane at the vehicle centreline. Any capture carrying it is self-calibrating even if the harness metadata is lost.

The other half of the answer is that the reference drawings’ rules are what matter, and those are gold. Manufacturer drawings carry dimension extension lines marking the exact measured points. Read the calibration off those, never off the drawing’s bounding box.

07

What makes geometry stay correct

Three findings, and they are what separates a model that converges from one that keeps regressing. They are about construction, not tooling.

a · One surface plus shader cuts beats several surfaces made to meet

Separate ribbons that have to be aligned will mis-register. Always. They mis-registered here at the A‑pillar, at the door surrounds and at the rear lamps, repeatedly, in different ways.

The fix that ended it: build one surface and remove what should not be there, with the aperture defined once and emitted into both the beauty material and the G‑buffer material from the same description. The A, B, C and D pillars are then not four parts that must be made to meet — they are what is left of a single skin after the windows are cut out. They cannot mis-register because there is nothing to register. The same finding in a different form at the rear: one band swept along the body’s own skin, around the corner and across the tail, instead of a flat panel plus two corner pieces.

Rule If two surfaces must meet exactly, they should be one surface.

b · A cut is not the same thing as its surface

The subtlest bug class in the build, and it appeared three separate times.

Panels are offset along the section normal to sit proud of or inset into the skin: +4 mm for a window surround, −2 mm for glass. The aperture test, however, runs on the fragment’s world position. Near a fillet the normal has a large vertical component, so a surface offset 4 mm outward has its cut edge land 4 mm higher than the surface it is supposed to align with. Three separate 4 mm slivers of daylight came from exactly this.

Rule Every panel filling an aperture must overlap it on all four edges by more than the largest normal offset in play. Flush is not good enough; flush is a sliver.

c · A constant is not a datum

beltY = 1.200 was used as the belt line. But the section’s own floor is ZT(x) − 12 mm, and ZT rises to 1.225 behind the doors. So at the rear quarter the aperture cut 13 mm below the glass it was supposed to frame, and no amount of adjusting the glass could close it.

The same mistake, in the same file, made the deck cut eat the foot of the windscreen: a flat ceiling of 1.26 m is under the roofline over the cabin but above the whole greenhouse section forward of x ≈ 0.92, so it removed the glass instead of the floor beneath it.

Rule A boundary that interacts with a swept surface must be a function of the same curve that swept it, not a number that happens to be right in the middle.

Where the boundary lives in a shader, sample the curve at build time and emit it as a piecewise-linear ramp — the technique already used for the hood’s shut line, and it is cheap:

deckCeil: linspace(0.58, 1.02, 0.02).map(x => [x, topYatZ(upperSec, x, DECK_Z) - 0.002]),

Note which curve. The first attempt tracked the centreline roof and still opened a slot, because the cut’s outboard edge is at |z| = 0.84 where the section is lower. Track the curve at the boundary’s own location.

d · An outward offset needs a direction, not a sign

The rear lamp is one band swept along the body’s own skin — the construction in (a), and the right one. It offsets itself off the skin by a scalar: along the flanks as p.z + normal.z * off, and across the tail as x_cap + off.

Along the flanks that is correct. Across the tail it is exactly backwards, because the vehicle’s x axis points forward, so “outward” at the tail is −x. Every band therefore sat off millimetres inside the tail skin, behind the liftgate panel, and the three bands stacked in reverse — the brow, meant to be proudest, ended up deepest. The user’s word for it was “inside out”, which is precisely what it was.

Rule An offset is a vector, not a number. The moment a swept surface turns a corner, a scalar that meant “outward” on one face means “inward” on the next.

e · A fix verified in one state can be a defect in another

The four-millimetre slivers of (b) were closed by leaving a 22 mm band of body uncut across the belt. That closed every hole, passed the hole detector, and was invisible in every capture taken — because every capture had the doors shut.

With the doors open it is a solid rail across both door openings, running the length of the cabin. A car cannot have one. It shipped, and the user found it in about a minute.

Rule A part that moves has to be checked in every state it moves through. A hole detector is not evidence about a state it never rendered.

The repair also shows the shape of the right fix: instead of the body keeping material to back the door, the door’s own upper panel now spans the whole door rather than just the glazed opening — which is what a real door does, since the window frame belongs to the door. Everything outside the body’s aperture is covered by the surround anyway, so the wider panel is invisible except where it should be. The aperture is then cut continuously, and there is nothing left to bar the doorway.

f · One defect masks another

With the lamp bands buried behind the liftgate, a second fault in them could not be seen: the brow’s height is set as tailY + 0.068, and the roll pulls the section’s top down to 1.178 at the tail, so the brow was asking for a height 5 mm above the bodywork. The height lookup silently clamped to the top corner and returned a crossing 60 mm narrower at each end than the strip below it — a third instance of (c), in the same file, found only because the first fix made it visible.

It was not found by looking, and not by measuring the render — the render could not show it. It came from an adversarial reviewer told to attack the proposed fix rather than the defect, which recomputed the section arithmetic and predicted the symptom before it existed.

Rule After fixing an occlusion, re-examine what it was hiding. When a fix changes what is visible, an adversarial pass is worth more than another screenshot.
08

Make the model agent-legible

The model should be queryable by the agent building it, not only renderable. This build ships a WebMCP surface exposing 13 tools; the ones that matter for reconstruction work:

Tools that remove a round trip
ToolWhy it removes a round trip
list_partsFull component listing with bounding boxes in metres — the component/coordinate/tag listing that otherwise has to be inferred from screenshots
get_partOne component’s box, centre, size, explode vector and engineering description
measureExact centre-to-centre distance; also the harness self-test of finding 03
frame_partPoints the camera at a component and fits it — replaces guessing an azimuth and re-shooting
set_cameraAbsolute and relative poses including an orthographic toggle, so a measurement view is reproducible by value
get_specificationThe published figures every curve is fitted to

Two design notes learned by getting them wrong. Return structured JSON, never prose — “the battery is about 2.8 metres long” has to be parsed and can be misparsed; {"size":{"x":2.84,"y":0.155,"z":1.56}} cannot. And errors should carry the recovery: no part "flux capacitor". Call list_parts for the 42 available ids. costs one round trip, where undefined costs three.

A third, found while testing: frame_part on an internal component puts the camera inside the body shell, which renders as noise. The tool now returns a hint telling the caller to dissolve the shell first.

Rule Any tool that can put the caller in an unrenderable state should say so in its own result.
09

The kernel

The recurring shape of this work is that a handful of primitives, defined once, generate the entire vehicle; everything else is data. Extracted from this build, the kernel is six layers in a strict dependency order.

CurveNamed 1‑D profiles over the long axis — roof height, belt height, floor height, half-widths at three heights, corner radii, crown. Monotone-cubic through hand-placed knots, because knots are what a measurement produces and monotone interpolation will not overshoot between them. All fitting happens here; the only layer that touches reference imagery.
SectionOne function section(x) returning a closed loop, built from the curves, with a fixed index map so “the top-right corner” is the same index at every station. That fixed map is what makes every downstream operation composable.
SurfaceFour operators over sections, sufficient for an entire vehicle: loft (sweep along x), colGrid (a ribbon between two index bounds), rowGrid (a ribbon between two heights), wrapBand (follow the skin around a corner). Plus capFromLoop to close an end.
ApertureOne declarative description of every hole, emitted as GLSL and compiled into every material that needs it. One source of truth; a door opening cannot disagree with itself.
AssemblyNamed parts with a category, rest position, explode vector, callout anchor and description. This is what the agent surface exposes, and it is nearly free once parts are named.
InstrumentSilhouette mode, part isolation, deterministic capture, hole detector, fit check. Built at the same time as the geometry, not after it.

The value of the kernel is that the strict order means a measurement correction only ever edits the curve layer. When the windscreen base moved 111 mm, one knot list changed and the pillars, surrounds, glass, cowl and wipers all followed — because the pillar edge is derived from the roof curve rather than being a separately fitted number. The failures that cost the most time were all places where that discipline had leaked: a constant standing in for a curve, or two surfaces fitted independently.

Proposed Skill shape

Phases, in order
PhaseWhat happens
0 · InstrumentsSilhouette mode, part isolation, deterministic capture, hole detector, fit check, axle-calibrated measurement with its wheelbase self-test. Refuse to start modelling until measure(front axle → rear axle) returns the published wheelbase.
1 · Reference triageCollect sources, rank them, and run the internal-consistency check on each. Record which are trusted for dimensions, which for topology only, and which are excluded.

4 · The measured loopAfter every edit: fit check, then hole detector, then — only then — a batched capture set for a visual read. Escalate to part isolation the moment a defect cannot be attributed to a specific named surface with certainty.
5 · AcceptanceFit within tolerance, zero interior see-through pixels, no console errors, determinism verified by capturing the same frame twice and comparing bytes, and a responsive check at real device viewports.
Caveat Headless Chrome silently clamps --window-size to a 500 px minimum, so “mobile” captures taken that way are not mobile. Real device emulation (375×812, 768×1024) is required, asserting scrollWidth === innerWidth and zero overflowing elements.
11

Things I would do differently

  • Build the hole detector and the fit check first. Both are around thirty lines, and both would have caught their defects on the day they were introduced rather than several sessions later.
  • Put the wheelbase self-test in the measurement harness from the beginning. A measurement tool with no self-test produced a confident, precise, wrong answer — and I believed it.
  • Treat “two surfaces need to meet” as a design smell on sight, not after the third mis-registration.
  • Never judge a fine-detail question from a downscaled screenshot. Two apparent catastrophes were moiré from a small viewport, and one apparent success was hiding a 12.8 mm hole behind a bright lip.
  • Check every moving part in its moved state before calling a fix done. The door bar passed every numeric check I had, because every check rendered the doors shut.
  • Distrust any measurement that contradicts a spec the model was built from. Both times that happened here — roof 15 mm low, windscreen base 111 mm forward — the answer was not the obvious one: once the measurement was wrong, once the model was, and only re-deriving the calibration told me which.
12

A measurement is only as good as the argument that the quantity moves

Findings 12–14 were earned on a different build — the AutoLab configurator and its agent surface, which embeds this drawing. Same discipline, different material. That they transfer at all is finding 13.

Finding A number can be real, correctly read, and about the wrong thing. Measuring the wrong quantity fails silently and confidently, which is worse than looking.

Testing whether touch drag orbits the vehicle, I asserted on the CSS variable the canvas uses for its pan. It read 0% before the drag and 0% after, on phone and on tablet. A clean, reproducible, two‑platform FAIL — and I almost reported it.

The variable is the authored still’s pan. It is deliberately frozen when the live renderer owns the drag, because the 3D camera controller handles the gesture inside the canvas instead. The instrument was pointed at a quantity that is inert by design in exactly the state under test. Re‑run against the rendered picture — difference the stage before and after — it passed on both platforms and on both the WebGL and the no‑WebGL path.

This is the failure mode that measurement introduces and looking does not. A screenshot that shows nothing moving is ambiguous and you know it. A number that says 0% → 0% reads as evidence.

Rule Every harness needs a positive control, not only a self‑test. Before trusting a null result, prove the instrument moves when the thing it measures moves — drive the change by a route already known to work, and watch the number respond. A harness that has never been seen to fail for the right reason has not been calibrated.

Finding 03 says a measurement tool with no self-test produced a confident, precise, wrong answer about a dimension. This is the same law one level up: the self-test proves the instrument is accurate, the positive control proves it is connected.

The same law bit again one level further out, and it is worth separating because the guard is different. A touch‑drag check reported “nothing moved” on a 390 px phone and passed on every other device. The instrument was right and the quantity was right: at that width the drag origin, chosen geometrically as the centre of the stage, landed on a view button — and the canvas deliberately ignores a drag that starts on a control. A true measurement of a gesture the app is correct to refuse. The fix is not a better number but a checked stimulus: search for a point provably clear of controls, and refuse to run if there is not one.

Rule Assert the preconditions of a measurement, not only its result. An instrument should fail loudly when it cannot set up the conditions it is measuring under, rather than quietly measuring something else.

The general form, and the cheapest guard: when a check can only ever return one value, it is not a check. Prefer instruments that read the artefact itself — pixels, geometry, the returned object — over proxies for it. A proxy is a second model of the system, and it can be wrong independently.

13

The law is not about geometry

Finding “Measure, don’t look” holds wherever the thing being judged is an artefact rather than an argument. For code, reading it is looking; running it is measuring.

The AutoLab work had no geometry to fit. The questions were whether an agent could use a tool surface, and whether a page degraded honestly. Every defect that survived review survived because it was being judged by reading the source.

Defects that reading did not catch and running did
DefectWhy reading missed itWhat caught it
WebGL detector stricter than the renderer it gates Both call sites are correct in isolation; the defect is that they disagree Blocking WebGL and reading what the page then claimed
The visible fallback image hidden from assistive tech The aria-hidden expression reads as careful Loading with no WebGL and querying the attribute
Agent told it was looking at the R2 while a different car was on screen The publishing code is right; it is called once, too early Asserting the published value in the no‑WebGL state
Body shell and glazing never wireframed in blueprint mode The loop is obviously correct — over the wrong collection Two reviewers independently tracing what the collection held
Zoom unreachable on touch A wheel listener looks like zoom support Dispatching real touch events and differencing the frame
Rule Extend phase 0 past geometry. Before judging a surface, build the instrument that exercises it in the states that matter — degraded, hostile, small, touch‑only, offline — and make the first call after any change a run, not a read.

One asymmetry worth naming, because it is the reason this is a separate finding rather than a restatement of 01: in geometry the measurement is cheaper than the look. Here it is dearer — a browser, a build, a harness — which is exactly why the temptation to read the code instead is strong, and why the instrument has to exist before the question is asked.

14

An instrument that does not survive the session is not phase 0

Finding Instruments were built first every time, and thrown away every time. Phase 0 was satisfied and its benefit was not compounding.

Five measurement harnesses were written in a single day on the AutoLab build, used once, and deleted — several of them re‑deriving the same capability from scratch hours apart.

Harnesses written, used and discarded in one session
InstrumentWhat it answeredFate
Deterministic multi-view captureDoes the body read correctly from every presetRewritten four times
Part isolation by material tintWhich part is that artefactDiscarded, then needed again
Degradation harnessTouch, reduced motion, no WebGL, four viewportsDeleted, rewritten same day
Frame differencerDid the picture change at allInlined, then lost
Social-card captureIs the shipped preview the right vehicleDeleted

The cost is not the writing — each is thirty to eighty lines. The cost is that a discarded instrument takes its calibration with it. The part‑isolation harness had already been built and thrown away once by the time it was needed to settle the wheel‑arch artefact, which by then had survived five rounds of looking.

Rule An instrument ships in the repository beside the thing it measures, is runnable by one command, and carries its own self‑test and positive control. If it is worth writing to answer a question once, it is worth committing — the second question is already on its way.

This is the practical bridge to the Skill in finding 10. Phase 0 should not read “build the instruments”; it should read “install the instruments, from the kit, and prove each one responds”. The kit is the deliverable. Everything else in this document is a conclusion the kit would have reached faster.
